I traced all three across every module. Important correction to the premise: **none of these three actually go through the Expense → `ManagerNoteService.processExpenseRequestTodo()` path in this codebase.** All three create `PayrollManagerNote` directly. No `setSalaryAdditionType(...)` call anywhere resolves to `prorated_salary`, `mv_prorated_salary`, or `last_day_cc_switch_adjustment`. The expense-code path exists and is real (it copies `Expense.salaryAdditionType` into `additionReason`), but it is not the producer of these three reasons. Here's the full trace.

---

## 1. `prorated_salary` ("Prorated salary")

**What creates it:** `_ProratedSalariesTransaction.calculate()` — a "phase-zero" salary transaction auto-discovered by reflection and run inside `HousemaidPayrollPaymentServiceV2.runTransactionsNew()` during **monthly payroll file generation**. Direct `PayrollManagerNoteRepository.save()`, addition reason set to the `prorated_salary` picklist item.

**When it runs:** During each monthly payroll generation pass (primary run, `!isSecondary`). Skips maids who already have a transferred payroll log for that month.

**Eligibility:** Salary start date (`replacementSalaryStartDate` if set, else `startDate`) is **on/after the 27th of the previous payroll month** and **before the 1st of the current payroll month** — i.e. maids who started in the last few days of the prior month and so were missed by that month's file.

**Amount formula — branches by housemaid type:**
- **MAID_VISA:** `round( (basicSalary / daysInPreviousMonth) × daysBetween(startDate, 1st-of-payroll-month) )`
- **CC / all others:** group-based proration — for each salary group (gr1 basic, gr2, gr4, gr5 live-out basic, gr6 live-out accommodation), `(groupSalary / daysInPreviousMonth) × groupDaysInPreviousMonth`, summed. Day counts per group come from `PayrollGroupService.createPayrollMonthlyGroup()` over `[startDate … last day of previous month]`.

**Note date:** set to `payrollStart` or the 1st of the payroll month (a pure calendar date → **stored at midnight**). This matches your production fact of **100% midnight** for prorated_salary. Because it's generated by the payroll engine (no human requester), that also fits.

## 2. `mv_prorated_salary` ("MV Prorated Salary")

**What creates it:** `AsyncService.processCurrentMonthHousemaidsBatchBT()` in payroll — direct `PayrollManagerNote` save with addition reason `mv_prorated_salary`. It is a **completely separate pipeline** from `prorated_salary`, not a contract-type branch of the same job.

**Full chain:**
1. A **Last MV Salary** service is created for a maid on a **pre-collected Maid-Visa contract** that is being cancelled/terminated (`MaidService` type `LAST_MV_SALARY_FOR_A_PRE_COLLECTED_CONTRACT`), via UI/API/GPT in housemaid-management. `salary` is captured from the maid's payroll history (company/basic salary).
2. Daily scheduled job **`LastMvSalaryMaidServiceJob`** (`create_last_mv_salary_todo_job`, cron in DB) flips it to `READY_TO_BE_PAID` on the termination day and asks payroll to build a dedicated single-maid WPS accountant todo (`AccountantToDoService.createAccountantTodoForTerminatedProratedMVMaids`).
3. When that WPS transfer is authorized, `AsyncService` processes the log and — **only if the maid is still active** — converts it into the `mv_prorated_salary` manager note.

**When it runs:** Event-driven off **WPS transfer completion** (not the monthly generation pass, and not a fixed cron).

**Eligibility:** Maid is `MAID_VISA` (or "MV switched to CC"), on a **pre-collected MV contract** that is cancelled / scheduled-for-termination in the window, client payment covers the salary — **and** at transfer time the housemaid is **NOT** `EMPLOYEMENT_TERMINATED` / `VISA_UNSUCCESSFUL`. Terminated maids get a plain WPS payment with no such note.

**Amount formula:** `note.amount = log.getTotalSalary()`, where the log's salary was computed upstream as:
`round( (maidService.salary / daysInTerminationMonth) × lastPaidDate.dayOfMonth )` — i.e. company/basic salary prorated over days worked from the 1st of the termination month through `lastPaidDate`.

**Note date:** `new Date()` = the actual processing timestamp → **not midnight**, and no human requester (system-generated during batch). This matches your facts: **0% midnight, 100% no requester**, and all activity in the last 12 months (recent feature).

## 3. `last_day_cc_switch_adjustment` ("Last Day CC Switch Adjustment") — brand new

**Trigger event:** Not a client switching *to* CC. It fires when a **CC (non-MV) maid is switched to Maid Visa**. Creating a Maid-Visa contract in Sales (`ContractController.createContract`) for a housemaid whose prior `HousemaidType` was not `MAID_VISA` calls `bindContractWithMaidVisaHousemaid()` (flips type to `MAID_VISA`) and `createCcSwitchedToMvRecord()`, persisting a `CcMaidSwitchedToMv` row with `switchDate = now` and `lastCcSalary = basicSalary`.

**What creates the note:** `PayrollAuditTodoService.doMaidSwitchedToMvCalculations()` — direct `PayrollManagerNote` save, addition reason `last_day_cc_switch_adjustment` (constant `PICKLIST_ITEM_LAST_DAY_CC_SWITCH_ADJUSTMENT_CODE`).

**When it runs:** The daily **`PayrollAuditTodoJob`** → on the payroll lock date enqueues background task `createCcSwitchingToMvTodo` → `doMaidSwitchedToMvCalculations` for each switched maid in the window. Can also run on-demand when an auditor opens the "CC switching to MV" audit todo (`PayrollAuditTodoController.getCcSwitchedToMvMaidsInfo`, if `alreadyCalculated == false`).

**Eligibility (all must hold):**
- A `CcMaidSwitchedToMv` record in the payroll window.
- **Switch date == the last calendar day of the payroll month** (`switchDate.equals(monthEnd)`).
- `lastCcSalary > 0` and computed daily rate `> 0`.
- Not excluded by "Rule 2" (switch within first 3 days of its month and on/after prior primary payment date → whole maid excluded).

**Amount formula:** one day of CC salary — `round( lastCcSalary / daysInPayrollMonth )`. Rationale in code: the main CC proration (`getSalaryForCcSwitchingToMv`) only pays through `switchDate − 1`, so the switch day itself is added back as this one-day adjustment because its note date falls outside the standard lock window.

**Note date:** set to the actual `switchDate` (last day of month). First notes ~2026-06-30 and only ~213 rows fit the "brand new" fact; the picklist item isn't even in the test seed data.

## Difference between `prorated_salary` and `mv_prorated_salary`

It is **not** just a contract-type flag on one job — they are two different jobs/flows:

| | `prorated_salary` | `mv_prorated_salary` |
|---|---|---|
| Business case | Maid **started after the 26th** of previous month; catch-up for the partial prior month | **Pre-collected MV contract cancelled**; pay for days worked in the termination month |
| Producer | `_ProratedSalariesTransaction` (phase-zero, monthly payroll generation) | `AsyncService.processCurrentMonthHousemaidsBatchBT` (post-WPS-transfer) |
| Trigger | Monthly payroll file generation | Last-MV-salary WPS transfer completion (via `LastMvSalaryMaidServiceJob`) |
| Applies to | Any maid (MV and CC branches in same method) | MV / MV-switched-to-CC pre-collected contracts only |
| Salary field | `basicSalary` (MV) or gr1–gr6 group salaries (CC) | company/basic salary snapshotted on `MaidService.salary` |
| Date basis | start date vs. previous payroll month | `lastPaidDate` (termination date) |
| Note date | 1st-of-month (midnight) | processing timestamp (non-midnight) |

The MV branch inside `_ProratedSalariesTransaction` also uses `basicSalary`, but that branch still produces the **`prorated_salary`** reason, not `mv_prorated_salary`. So the two reasons never come from the same code path — the distinguishing factor is the **triggering event** (late-start monthly proration vs. pre-collected-contract cancellation payout), not merely contract type.

One note on the original hypothesis: `ManagerNoteService.processExpenseRequestTodo()` genuinely does copy `Expense.salaryAdditionType` → `PayrollManagerNote.additionReason`, so other addition reasons in production do arrive that way — but for these three specific codes, no expense is ever stamped with them, so the direct-creation paths above are the complete story.
