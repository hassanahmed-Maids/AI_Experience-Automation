## 🔴 What this report could not have found, and the live runs did

The spec audit was a review of a document. Three findings only a run could produce, and each changes
how the spec should be read:

1. **Current state is not history.** Six findings rested on a current-state column standing in for a
   historical fact, and every one moved when a log replaced it — airfare 137,500 → **4,500**, bonus
   143,965 → candidates, raffle 3,000 → **0**, relocation 4,700 → 3,900, anti-attrition 2,476 →
   **5,726**, office work *a clear* → **void**. Now §1b of v3 and §5b of the DEV spec.
2. **The reference lists the spec asks a person for already exist in the database.**
   `EXPENSES_CONFIGURATION` declares category, approval method, limit, invoice requirement and loan
   flag for all 31 salary-addition heads. `HOUSEMAID_TYPE_LOGS` is N17. `LIVE_OUT` on the type log is
   N19. **Three open business asks were answered by two queries.**
3. **The withdrawal rate is the story.** Fourteen candidate findings were raised and retracted;
   confirmed findings total ~AED 103,100 against roughly twenty times that withdrawn. Every finding
   built on a proxy shrank when the real classifier arrived; **none grew**. The ones that held were
   measured directly against a rule the code states.

## CRITICAL — S1's `both NULL → RED` manufactures ~850 findings a year

**Where:** §3 M6, the S1 block (line 1062).
**Claim:** *"`LOWER(REQUESTED_BY) = LOWER(APPROVED_BY)` → RED; both NULL on a human-entered type → RED."*

**Why it fails.** Measured over 12 months, 862 notes in the human-type list have neither a requester
nor an approver — and **850 of them are `Bonus`**. §4 of the corroboration design records, code-verified,
that the retraction half of `Bonus` is machine-created through
`DelighterService.handleRetractDelighterWithOneTimeBonus → addExpenseRequestForHousemaid →
ManagerNoteService.processExpenseRequestTodo`. Machine-created notes carry null attribution **by
design**. So the rule as written RED-flags a design behaviour, on the largest single block it will
ever see, in its first month.

This is trap #2 inverted: a test that cannot distinguish a defect from a design decision. It is also
trap #5 — *"human-entered type"* is a hardcoded prose list with no source and no test.

**Fix.** Two changes.
1. Determine machine-vs-human **empirically per payment type**, not from a list. Batch days are
   observable (`GROUP BY NOTE_DATE::DATE HAVING COUNT(*) > n`), and the attributed notes of a type
   are a within-type control group for its unattributed ones (§3r).
2. `both NULL` → **RED only where the type is measurably human**; **BLOCKED** where origin is
   unresolved. Bonus is currently unresolved: concentration ruled out a batch job but cannot
   separate event-driven automation from a person (§3r).

**Verified.** Query 1d, 12-month window, 10 human types: 862 unattributed, 850 of them Bonus.

## CRITICAL — S1 has no time window, in a spec where every other metric is monthly

**Where:** §3 M6 S1, against §3 M0 (`AUDIT_MONTH`).

**Why it fails.** Every other metric is anchored to `M0.AUDIT_MONTH`. S1 as written spans the whole
table. The consequence is not cosmetic: **79% of the unattributed money is older than 12 months**,
and Salary Dispute's unattributed block falls from 4,211 notes all-time to **12 notes / AED 3,484**
in the last twelve months. An un-windowed S1 on the same dashboard as windowed metrics reports a
mostly-closed historical backlog as a live control failure, and points the auditor at the wrong
month's work.

**Fix.** Window S1 to `AUDIT_MONTH` like everything else, and publish the historical backlog
separately as a one-off remediation scope, not as a monthly finding.

**Verified.** Query 1d vs the un-windowed S1: 7,147 → 862 notes, AED 2.86m → AED 613,759.

## MAJOR — a control is *worsening* and the spec has no way to say so

**Where:** §1 Business Logic; §3 M7–M11 (all point-in-time counts).

**Why it fails.** `Bonus` attribution ran at **2.2%** unattributed before 2024-05, **24.5%** through
onset, **85.4%** at peak, and **48.2%** now. Taxi Reimbursement is **0% across 36 months**. These are
different situations and every metric in v2 renders them as one number in one month. A monthly count
cannot distinguish "a control that has always been weak", "a backlog being worked off" and "a control
that broke 28 months ago and is still broken" — and only the third needs an owner this week.

**Fix.** Add a trend obligation: any control-failure metric publishes its 24-month monthly series
beside the current value. §4's UI has room; this is one sparkline per finding class.

**Verified.** Query 1e, 36 months, three types.

## MAJOR — no chance baseline is required for any window-based test

**Where:** §3 M3 T6 (duplicate windows), M4 (entitlement window), and every group rule that looks
for a corroborating record within a period.

**Why it fails.** A test of the form *"is there a related record within N days"* has a hit rate that
owes nothing to the business: if the window spans W days and "close enough" covers C of them, one
record lands inside by geometry alone with p = C/W. Measured on the complaint layer, a 105-day
window with a 30-day proximity band gives **p = 0.286**, and at 1.7 related records per subject
chance alone produces a **40% hit rate**. The observed rate for anti-attrition was 40.3% — **1.00×
chance, i.e. zero signal** — while reading as "40% corroborated" on a dashboard.

**Fix.** Any window-based test must publish its chance rate next to its observed rate, computed
per subject from that subject's own record count (the cohort average is a Jensen trap: estimating it
rather than measuring it produced errors of 1.46× vs 2.33× in a real case). Where a null cohort
exists — a random draw, a lottery, a scheduled batch — carry it as a control group.

**Verified.** Queries 6a-ii / 6a-iii; §3g.

## MAJOR — the ERP-job assumption "runs at month end" is false one month in twelve

**Where:** §3 M0 branch 2 (lock windows); anywhere a batch is inferred from a date.

**Why it fails.** The anti-attrition batch ran on **2026-09-01**, not 2026-08-31 — the largest batch
in the series, 918 notes. Any rule that identifies machine-created notes by "last calendar day of the
month" misfiles all of them. A related defect is already proven in this codebase: `NOTE_DATE` carries
a time, so `NOTE_DATE = LAST_DAY(NOTE_DATE)` is **false for every row** and returns a well-formed,
plausible, entirely meaningless split.

**Fix.** State once, in §3, that batch run days are **observed from the data**, never assumed from
the calendar, and that any date equality against a `DATE` function must cast `NOTE_DATE::DATE` first.

**Verified.** Query 6g and its sanity check; §3l, §3m.

## MAJOR — S1 compares two columns drawn from different sources

**Where:** §3 M6 S1; §2.1 D7 / D16.

**Why it fails.** `REQUESTED_BY` is populated from `mmdb.users.FULL_NAME` — canonical
`"Firstname Lastname"`. `APPROVED_BY` is free text, a name and not a user id, and the numeric
`MANAGER` column that would settle identity is dead (§2.1, already noted). **43% of the approvals
that exist carry a bare first name**, and whitespace is not clean (`"Georgina  Wakim"` carries a
double space, so it fails a naive equality against `"Georgina Wakim"`).

**Honest scope.** I predicted this was hiding self-approvals as mismatched name forms and **tested
it: the name-form class returns 0 on every payment type.** The current miss rate is zero. The defect
is robustness, not a live under-count — but the comparison matches by luck rather than by design, and
one normalisation change upstream would alter the result.

**Fix.** Normalise case *and* internal whitespace; state that a bare first name resolves to a person
only when it is unique among staff; where it is not, BLOCKED — and **only where the names actually
match**, since a first name that differs from the requester's is a different person under every
reading of it. (Over-blocking is not the safe direction: blocking all 7,804 single-token approvals
would bury the findings and inflate the same denominator.)

**Verified.** Rewritten S1 (1c and query 1); §3o, §3p, §3q.

## MAJOR — the largest payment type by volume has no group rule that can run

**Where:** §3 M6 group B (`anti_attrition_incentive`).

**Why it fails.** Anti-attrition is **9,167 notes a year — roughly 59% of the audited population by
count** (AED 1.83m, 29% of the money). Four of its candidate checks are now closed off by evidence:

| Check | Status |
|---|---|
| Corroborating complaint | **N_A.** 1.00× chance — zero signal (§3g) |
| Enrolment record exists | Passes 999 times in 1,000 (§3h) — a control that always fires |
| Amount = tier | **Cannot be written.** 30%+ of notes are prorated, over two different divisors, both the job's own (§3j, §3m) |
| Amount fits no rule | **Withdrawn.** Detects whole-dirham typing, not error (§3o) |

**Fix.** Group B's rule is **Job 1 — the AI Agent reads `HOUSEMAID_MANAGERACTIONLOGS.NOTES` and judges
whether it states a retention reason.** That is now evidenced as viable: the box is **100% filled,
96% distinct values, 1% on the commonest value, median 43 chars** (§3s). Two live RED tests exist
alongside it: `ENROLLED_AFTER_PAYMENT` (an enrolment dated after the payment it justifies — already
found one, note 184233) and the double-pay-in-one-month review list. The recompute stays **BLOCKED**
on a named ask: enrolment **and exit** dates, not just `INCENTIVE_AMOUNT`.

**Verified.** Queries 1f, 6a-iii, 6d, 6g, 6h.

## MINOR — future-dated notes are not rejected

One `Bonus` note is dated **2026-10**, a month that has not happened. M0 branch 3 would amber it as
*"paid month cannot be established"*, which is the right destination by luck rather than by rule.
State it explicitly: a note dated after the audit month is a data defect, not an unverifiable case.

## MINOR — §1's population figures understate the concentration

*"roughly 1,300 additions … some AED 6.3m a year across ~16,000 payments"* is right in total, but
anti-attrition alone is 9,167 of those notes. **One payment type is the majority of the population
by count and a minority by money**, which is the single most useful orientation fact for a reader and
it is not stated.
